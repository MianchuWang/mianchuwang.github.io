Sampling from the full distribution lets the long tail of near-zero tokens leak in. Top-p (nucleus) sampling keeps the smallest set of most-likely tokens whose probabilities already sum to at least p, throws away the rest, and renormalises.

  • probs — (..., vocab_size), each row a distribution summing to 1
  • p — the nucleus threshold, e.g. 0.9

Return an array of the same shape: kept tokens hold their renormalised probability, dropped tokens are exactly 0, and every row still sums to 1. The most likely token is always kept, even when its probability alone exceeds p. Positions must stay where they were — sort internally, but do not return a sorted row.

Hint

np.argsort gives you the descending order (sort -probs), then np.take_along_axis / np.put_along_axis move a mask between sorted and original order. Keeping the token that crosses the threshold is the fiddly part: cumsum - sorted_probs < p does it, and keeps the top token for free.
