The inner loop of BPE training — the part worth being able to write cold. The corpus is already pre-tokenized into words; each word is a tuple of symbols with a count:

{("l","o","w"): 5, ("l","o","w","e","r"): 2, ("n","e","w","e","s","t"): 6, ...}

One training step does three things:

  • Count every adjacent symbol pair, weighted by the word's count — the pair ("l","o") in a word with count 5 contributes 5.
  • Pick the most frequent pair; break ties by taking the lexicographically greater pair.
  • Merge it in every word: each adjacent occurrence, scanning left to right without overlap, becomes one concatenated symbol — ("s","t") → ("st",).

Return (best_pair, merged_words) as a new dict — do not mutate the input. If two words become identical after the merge, their counts add up.

Hint

max(counts, key=lambda p: (counts[p], p)) gets the count-then-lexicographic ordering in one line. For the merge, a while i < len(word) scan is cleaner than fancy slicing: on a match append the concatenation and jump i += 2, otherwise copy one symbol. That jump is what makes ("a","a","a") merge into ("aa","a"), not ("aa","aa").
