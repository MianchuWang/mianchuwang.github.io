Training learned an ordered list of merges; encoding replays them. Given one pre-split word and the merge list, tokenize the word the way GPT-2's encoder does:

word   = ("l","o","w","e","s","t")
merges = [("s","t"), ("e","st"), ("o","w")]   # earlier = higher priority
→ ("l", "ow", "est")
  • Rank each pair by its position in merges — index 0 is the strongest merge.
  • Loop: among the adjacent pairs currently in the word, find the one with the lowest rank. No pair has a rank → done.
  • Merge every non-overlapping occurrence of that pair, scanning left to right — same scan as the training step — then repeat.

The loop matters: applying a merge can create a pair with an even earlier rank, so one pass over merges is not enough. Symbols never seen in training simply stay as they are.

Hint

Build rank = {pair: i for i, pair in enumerate(merges)} once, then each round take min(pairs, key=lambda p: rank.get(p, float("inf"))) over the pairs present in the word; stop when the winner is not in rank at all. The merge itself is the while i < len(symbols) scan you already wrote for the training step — append the concatenation and jump i += 2 on a match.
